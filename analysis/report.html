Purpose

Cyclistic’s finance team treats annual members as more profitable than casual riders (single-ride and day passes). A monitoring product should show how the two programmes are used, with uncertainty and data quality, not only a pair of bar charts.

This report uses five calendar years of public Divvy trip files (January 2021–December 2025). The unit is the trip. There is no rider identifier, so we do not estimate unique participants or “coverage.”

The original student analysis (2019–2020 quarterly files, mean ride length, two plots) is in legacy/.

Methods

Cleaning rules, indicator definitions, Wilson intervals, STL, and the chi-square caveat are specified in docs/METHODS.md. Headline duration is the median, after dropping trips shorter than 60 seconds or longer than 24 hours.

Monthly files are processed independently; only indicator tables are combined. The dashboard reads those tables, not microdata.

What this would mean for a membership team

These are monitoring conclusions, not a campaign brief:

  1. Casual use is more weekend- and midday-shaped; member use is more weekday-shaped. That is now visible in the hour heatmap, not only in weekday totals.
  2. Casual trips are longer on the median, but the original analysis overstated the gap by using the mean of uncapped durations.
  3. Electric bikes are a large share of events; station-based metrics miss GPS-only trips if missing station names are dropped.

A registry-style next step (not possible here) would be a person key and a true participation rate.
